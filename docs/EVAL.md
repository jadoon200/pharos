# Evaluation

> The point of PHAROS is not that it *emits* maritime incidents — a few thresholds can do that.
> The point is that they are **measured**, and measured *honestly*.
>
> **Two evaluations, and the second is the one that counts.** (1) A **synthetic gold set** with
> labelled events gives per-detector precision/recall, a calibration trap, and a
> trajectory-anomaly AUC where the flagship model is compared against fair baselines. But
> self-generated anomalies are separable *by construction* — I author both the anomaly and the
> detector — so a high synthetic AUC is a **ceiling, not a capability claim**. (2) **Real NOAA
> Marine Cadastre AIS** is the honest test: data the model never got to define. There it
> immediately exposed a real weakness (catastrophic false-positive rates in congested ports), and
> the fix — measured before/after — is the actual result. Both are below; the real one is the point.

## What we measure

### 1. Per-incident-type detector quality (the ensemble)
Each detector covers a different maritime threat. Scored against a labelled gold set + Global
Fishing Watch event labels as a silver cross-check.

| Detector | Threat | Metric | Result (offline gold set) |
|---|---|---|---|
| gap | dark ship / AIS gap | precision / recall vs injected labels | 1.0 / 1.0 † |
| rendezvous | ship-to-ship transfer | precision / recall vs injected labels | 1.0 / 1.0 † |
| loiter | loitering / zone incursion | precision / recall vs injected labels | 1.0 / 1.0 † |
| spoof | impossible kinematics / identity | precision / recall (deterministic ground truth) | 1.0 / 1.0 |
| anomaly | trajectory deviation | ROC-AUC (GRU flagship, unsupervised) | 0.96 † |
| **ensemble** | **all types** | **per-type coverage (best detector each)** | **all 5 types covered** |

The live block under **Auto-recorded results** is regenerated by `make eval` (5 seeds). † The
detector P/R are near-perfect *by construction* — the synthetic events are separable — so they
demonstrate the harness, matching, and trap logic, **not** real-world difficulty (which the
**Real-data validation** section supplies). The confounders (benign anchorage, brief pass, legit
manoeuvre) give the deterministic detectors realistic false-alarm pressure, which they handle.

### 2. Trajectory-anomaly model — the flagship, and the one informative synthetic number
The flagship is a **GRU sequence autoencoder** (`detect/seq_anomaly.py`) over the ordered per-step
track, trained the honest way (benign train/val split, early stopping, a recorded learning curve).
It is compared against two baselines under the realistic **unsupervised** setup (train on *all*
tracks, no labels), and scored by AUC of the injected anomalies vs benign transits, both
within-region and cross-region (train Singapore, score US west coast — the region-agnostic shape
features make transfer possible):

| Model | Within-region AUC | Cross-region AUC |
|---|---|---|
| **GRU sequence-AE (flagship)** | **~0.96** | **~0.96** |
| PCA linear (baseline) | ~0.27 | — |

The informative result is not the GRU's absolute number (a synthetic ceiling) but the **gap**: the
linear PCA baseline falls *below chance* under unsupervised training on the confounder-rich
set, while the recurrent model holds up. The depth is necessary, not decorative. (Exact numbers in
the auto-recorded block.)

### 3. The AIS coverage confound (the honest limitation)
An apparent "dark ship" is often a benign receiver-coverage gap, not evasive behaviour. The gap
detector already requires displacement (a gap with ~0 movement is not flagged), the offline eval's
**coverage-gap trap** exercises exactly this (held 5/5 seeds), every incident carries an AIS
reliability grade, and GFW's reception-modelled gap events are the real-world cross-check.

| Metric | Definition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage-gap trap held | benign anchored-vessel gaps not called dark-ship | 5/5 seeds |
| Gap FP rate vs coverage (real data) | share of "dark" calls attributable to reception gaps | needs NOAA + GFW |
| Reliability calibration (real data) | do low-reliability incidents concentrate the false alarms? | needs NOAA + GFW |

## Real-data validation (the one that counts)

Run: `make eval-real FILE=data/ais/la_2020_01_01.csv REGION=us-la` on a real day of NOAA Marine
Cadastre AIS (LA / Long Beach approaches, 2020-01-01): **352 vessels, 145,028 position reports,
388 tracks** — data the model never got to define.

**Real behaviour, unprompted by any synthetic assumption:**
- **spoof: 5** — genuine impossible-speed jumps (60–85 kn implied), real GPS/AIS glitches, flagged `B`.
- **gap: 2** — e.g. a vessel dark for ~20 h with ~0 displacement, correctly graded **E** (low
  reliability — benign anchoring, not evasion). The reliability grading does its job on real data.

**The real weakness the synthetic set hid, and the fix (measured before → after):** a congested
port is full of anchored vessels sitting slow-and-near each other, which the naive detectors call
ship-to-ship transfers and loitering. Three domain-correct fixes, each traceable to this data:

| Detector | Naive | + anchored-vessel exclusion / anchoring speed-floor | + port-zone exclusion & partner-degree cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| rendezvous | **2,892** | 68 | **8** |
| loiter | **100** | **36** | 36 |
| **total incidents** | **2,999** | — | **51** |

A ~**98% false-positive reduction** to 51 review-worthy incidents. This is the actual engineering
result; the synthetic gold set could not have produced it because it has no realistic congestion.

**Flagship GRU on real pattern-of-life (unsupervised, no labels):** trained on the 388 real tracks
(real train/val split, early-stopped ~epoch 100), the most-anomalous tracks it surfaces are the
**Catalina Island high-speed ferries** ("SUPER EXPRESS", "CATALINA JET", "STARSHIP EXPRESS":
130–143 km high-speed shuttle runs) — genuinely the most distinctive pattern-of-life against the
anchor-and-berth cargo majority. Interpretable, real outliers. Raw AIS carries no anomaly *labels*
(GFW would provide silver labels via `pharos.eval.gfw_check`), so this lane is qualitative on real
data; the quantitative real result is the false-positive reduction above.

## Recorded negatives

- **Synthetic-eval ceiling.** On the gold set every detector scores ~1.0 P/R and the GRU ~0.96 AUC,
  because I author both the anomaly and the detector. This is a *ceiling*, not a capability claim.
  The linear PCA baseline collapsing below chance (~0.27) under unsupervised training is the one
  genuinely informative synthetic result — it shows the recurrent architecture is necessary.
- **Congested-port false positives (found on real data, fixed).** Naive rendezvous/loiter over-fired
  2,999→ before the anchored-vessel / port-zone / partner-degree fixes. A residual: 36 loiter and 8
  rendezvous incidents on the real day are surfaced for human review, not verified — without AIS
  identity/context PHAROS cannot certify intent, by design.
- **Anomaly detection is shape-only.** A covert detour and a legitimate reroute can be
  shape-identical; without context (why did it deviate?) they are not separable. The subtlest
  synthetic anomalies overlap benign manoeuvres for exactly this reason.
- **AIS coverage confound.** An apparent dark ship is often a receiver-coverage gap; handled via the
  displacement requirement, the reliability grade (real example: the 20 h gap graded E), the
  coverage-gap trap (held 5/5), and the GFW gap-event cross-check — not assumed away.
