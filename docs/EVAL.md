# Evaluation

> The point of PHAROS is not that it *emits* maritime incidents — a few thresholds can do that.
> The point is that they are **measured**, and measured *honestly*.
>
> **Two evaluations, and the second is the one that counts.** (1) A **synthetic gold set** with
> labelled events gives per-detector precision/recall, a calibration trap, and a
> trajectory-anomaly AUC where the flagship model is compared against fair baselines. But
> self-generated anomalies are separable *by construction* — I author both the anomaly and the
> detector — so a high synthetic AUC is a **ceiling, not a capability claim**. (2) **Real NOAA
> Marine Cadastre AIS** is the honest test: data the model never got to define. There it
> immediately exposed a real weakness (catastrophic false-positive rates in congested ports), and
> the fix — measured before/after — is the actual result. Both are below; the real one is the point.

## What we measure

### 1. Per-incident-type detector quality (the ensemble)
Each detector covers a different maritime threat. Scored against a labelled gold set + Global
Fishing Watch event labels as a silver cross-check.

| Detector | Threat | Metric | Result (offline gold set) |
|---|---|---|---|
| gap | dark ship / AIS gap | precision / recall vs injected labels | 1.0 / 1.0 † |
| rendezvous | ship-to-ship transfer | precision / recall vs injected labels | 1.0 / 1.0 † |
| loiter | loitering / zone incursion | precision / recall vs injected labels | 1.0 / 1.0 † |
| spoof | impossible kinematics / identity | precision / recall (deterministic ground truth) | 1.0 / 1.0 |
| anomaly | trajectory deviation | ROC-AUC (GRU flagship, unsupervised) | 0.971 † |
| **ensemble** | **all types** | **per-type coverage (best detector each)** | **all 5 types covered** |

The live block under **Auto-recorded results** is regenerated by `make eval` (5 seeds). † The
detector P/R are near-perfect *by construction* — the synthetic events are separable — so they
demonstrate the harness, matching, and trap logic, **not** real-world difficulty (which the
**Real-data validation** section supplies). The confounders (benign anchorage, brief pass, legit
manoeuvre) give the deterministic detectors realistic false-alarm pressure, which they handle.

### 2. Trajectory-anomaly model — the flagship, and the one informative synthetic number
The flagship is a **GRU sequence autoencoder** (`detect/seq_anomaly.py`) over the ordered per-step
track, trained on the unlabeled population with a held-out validation split, early stopping, and a
recorded learning curve.
It is compared against two baselines under the realistic **unsupervised** setup (train on *all*
tracks, no labels), and scored by AUC of the injected anomalies vs benign transits, both
within-region and cross-region (train Singapore, score US west coast — the region-agnostic shape
features make transfer possible):

| Model | Within-region AUC | Cross-region AUC |
|---|---|---|
| **GRU sequence-AE (flagship, 8 hidden)** | **0.971** | **0.967** |
| Isolation Forest (nonlinear baseline) | 0.940 | 0.942 |
| PCA linear (baseline) | ~0.27 | — |

The informative result is not the GRU's absolute number (a synthetic ceiling) but the **gap**: it
beats a nonlinear small-data baseline as well as linear PCA, which falls below chance under
unsupervised training on the confounder-rich set. A 25-run data-seed × initialization-seed capacity
sweep selected 8 hidden units over 16/32/64: 0.966 within / 0.967 cross-region mean AUC, lower
variance, and 804 parameters rather than 38,660 at 64 units. On the 493 real Gulf tracks, the new
and old rankings correlate 0.979 and share 19/25 top outliers; the compact model trains 2.4× faster.
Run `make benchmark-anomaly` to reproduce the capacity sweep independently of the normal eval.

This is the best **measured PHAROS fit**, not a universal architecture claim. Maritime
[GeoTrackNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00682) uses a probabilistic recurrent representation and
location-aware detection over 4.2M AIS messages; [TrAISformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03958)
targets long-horizon trajectory *prediction*, not whole-track anomaly ranking; and
[Anomaly Transformer](https://openreview.net/forum?id=LzQQ89U1qm_) targets generic point anomalies.
PHAROS has hundreds of unlabeled whole tracks, a 15-step representation, CPU/offline constraints,
and a cross-region objective. Under those conditions, a compact GRU is better supported than a
from-scratch Transformer; changing families requires a result on external trajectory-anomaly
labels, which are not currently available.

### 3. The AIS coverage confound (the honest limitation)
An apparent "dark ship" is often a benign receiver-coverage gap, not evasive behaviour. The gap
detector already requires displacement (a gap with ~0 movement is not flagged), the offline eval's
**coverage-gap trap** exercises exactly this (held 5/5 seeds), every incident carries an AIS
reliability grade, and GFW's reception-modelled gap events are the real-world cross-check.

| Metric | Definition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage-gap trap held | benign anchored-vessel gaps not called dark-ship | 5/5 seeds |
| GFW rendezvous corroboration (east-Gulf cohort) | vessel + type + 25 km + 24 h agreement | 4/34 PHAROS calls (11.8%) |
| GFW rendezvous corroboration (full east-Gulf population) | same rule, unbiased background population | 3/186 PHAROS calls (1.6%) |
| GFW loiter corroboration (east-Gulf cohort) | vessel + type + 25 km + 24 h agreement | 65/298 PHAROS calls (21.8%) |
| GFW gap corroboration (east-Gulf cohort) | same agreement rule | 0/47; GFW endpoint outside NOAA receiver coverage |
| Reliability calibration (real data) | do low-reliability incidents concentrate the false alarms? | still needs compatible independent gap labels |

## Real-data validation (the one that counts)

Run: `make eval-real FILE=data/ais/la_2020_01_01.csv REGION=us-la` on a real day of NOAA Marine
Cadastre AIS (LA / Long Beach approaches, 2020-01-01): **352 vessels, 145,028 position reports,
388 tracks** — data the model never got to define.

**Real behaviour, unprompted by any synthetic assumption:**
- **spoof: 5** — genuine impossible-speed jumps (60–85 kn implied), real GPS/AIS glitches, flagged `B`.
- **gap: 2** — e.g. a vessel dark for ~20 h with ~0 displacement, correctly graded **E** (low
  reliability — benign anchoring, not evasion). The reliability grading does its job on real data.

**The real weakness the synthetic set hid, and the fix (measured before → after):** a congested
port is full of anchored vessels sitting slow-and-near each other, which the naive detectors call
ship-to-ship transfers and loitering. Three domain-correct fixes, each traceable to this data:

| Detector | Naive | + anchored-vessel exclusion / anchoring speed-floor | + port-zone exclusion & partner-degree cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| rendezvous | **2,892** | 68 | **8** |
| loiter | **100** | **36** | 36 |
| **total incidents** | **2,999** | — | **51** |

A ~**98% false-positive reduction** to 51 review-worthy incidents. This is the actual engineering
result; the synthetic gold set could not have produced it because it has no realistic congestion.

**Flagship GRU on real pattern-of-life (unsupervised, no labels):** trained on the 388 real tracks
(real train/val split, early-stopped ~epoch 100), the most-anomalous tracks it surfaces are the
**Catalina Island high-speed ferries** ("SUPER EXPRESS", "CATALINA JET", "STARSHIP EXPRESS":
130–143 km high-speed shuttle runs) — genuinely the most distinctive pattern-of-life against the
anchor-and-berth cargo majority. Interpretable, real outliers. Raw AIS carries no anomaly *labels*;
GFW supplies event-level silver labels for three deterministic detectors, not trajectory-anomaly
labels. The anomaly lane therefore remains qualitative on real data; the quantitative real result
is the false-positive reduction above.

**Global Fishing Watch cross-check (recorded negative):** `make eval-real` queried the current GFW
v3 gap, encounter, and loitering datasets for the same 2020-01-01 LA/LB time and geographic window.
The API returned **0 candidate events**, so the resulting agreement was gap **0/2**, rendezvous
**0/8**, and loiter **0/36**. This is not evidence that all 46 PHAROS calls are false: the silver
label denominator is empty, GFW encounters cover a narrower set of vessel-type pairings, and its
loitering definition is offshore-oriented. It does mean this NOAA slice cannot support the planned
external-label precision estimate. A richer date/region must be selected before GFW calibration is
claimable; zero coverage is kept here rather than silently omitted. See the
[GFW Events API definitions](https://globalfishingwatch.org/our-apis/documentation).

### Externally labelled east-Gulf cohort — selected

The richer cross-check window is **2023-07-25, east Gulf of Mexico**, bounded to
`27.0–30.5°N, 93.0–88.0°W`. The exact GFW v3 query returned **1 gap, 4 encounter, and 283 loiter
events**. The full NOAA box contains 1,394,820 reports across 1,982 vessels. A transparent
label-enriched cohort keeps every GFW-named vessel
present in NOAA (including both sides of an encounter) plus 150 deterministically selected
background vessels: **337 vessels, 173,204 reports, 493 tracks**. This supports calibration and
matching, not population prevalence or an unbiased precision estimate.

`make eval-real` produced **379 PHAROS incidents**: gap 47, rendezvous 34, loiter 298, spoof 0.
Matching now requires the same public vessel identifier, incident type, ≤25 km, and ≤24 h:

| Detector | GFW events in selection query | PHAROS incidents | Corroborated | Agreement rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gap | 1 | 47 | 0 | 0.0% |
| rendezvous | 4 | 34 | 4 | 11.8% |
| loiter | 283 | 298 | 65 | 21.8% |

The denominator is PHAROS calls, but these rates are **not precision**: GFW is an incomplete silver
label with different event definitions, and the corpus is label-enriched. The useful result is that
69 real PHAROS calls independently agree at vessel/type/time/place level—unlike the LA/LB window,
which had no GFW labels at all.

#### Full-population rendezvous benchmark — scaling limit removed

The spatial candidate index now makes the complete box tractable without changing exact detector
semantics. It bounds each vessel's linearly interpolated **slow-motion** path in 30-minute bins,
inserts distance-expanded envelopes into coarse coordinate cells, and exact-resamples only pairs
sharing time and space. The index is conservative: false candidates are allowed; the original
5-minute speed, haversine-distance, duration, port, and partner-degree tests still make the call.

| Corpus / mode | Transit-eligible vessels | Possible pairs | Exact candidates | Rendezvous calls | Detector time* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| label-enriched cohort, exhaustive | 224 | 24,976 | 24,976 | 34 | 1.06s |
| label-enriched cohort, indexed | 224 | 24,976 | 881 (3.53%) | 34 | 0.67s |
| full east-Gulf population, indexed | 1,370 | 937,765 | 51,570 (5.50%) | 186 (93 pairs) | 7.12s |

\* Local CPU wall time after CSV parsing; timings are indicative. On the cohort, indexed and
exhaustive output matched exactly down to incident ids, timestamps, scores, and evidence. The
full run's live GFW cross-check corroborated **3/186 calls (1.6%)** against four encounter labels.
That is not precision: GFW is incomplete and definitionally narrower. The lower full-population
rate versus the label-enriched cohort is the honest denominator/context effect; the partner-degree
filter can also remove a pairing once the surrounding traffic population is restored.

The gap miss is a measured source-coverage incompatibility. GFW's labelled vessel stopped at
2023-07-25 18:36 UTC and reappeared on 2023-08-10, 169–209 km offshore. NOAA's national archives
contain its 680 reports up to two minutes before the gap, but no reappearance report—even on the
GFW endpoint day—because Marine Cadastre's terrestrial feed does not cover that offshore signal.
A search of all 38 Gulf GFW gaps in 2023 found no clean near-shore substitute: even the closest
candidate had at least one endpoint 94 km offshore. Gap precision therefore remains uncalibrated;
the failed overlap is recorded rather than converted into a false negative.

## Recorded negatives

- **Synthetic-eval ceiling.** On the gold set every detector scores ~1.0 P/R and the GRU ~0.97 AUC,
  because I author both the anomaly and the detector. This is a *ceiling*, not a capability claim.
  Beating Isolation Forest (0.940/0.942) and linear PCA (~0.27) is informative model-selection
  evidence, but it remains synthetic rather than an externally labelled capability estimate.
- **Congested-port false positives (found on real data, fixed).** Naive rendezvous/loiter over-fired
  2,999→ before the anchored-vessel / port-zone / partner-degree fixes. A residual: 36 loiter and 8
  rendezvous incidents on the real day are surfaced for human review, not verified — without AIS
  identity/context PHAROS cannot certify intent, by design.
- **Anomaly detection is shape-only.** A covert detour and a legitimate reroute can be
  shape-identical; without context (why did it deviate?) they are not separable. The subtlest
  synthetic anomalies overlap benign manoeuvres for exactly this reason.
- **AIS coverage confound.** An apparent dark ship is often a receiver-coverage gap; handled via the
  displacement requirement, the reliability grade (real example: the 20 h gap graded E), the
  coverage-gap trap (held 5/5), and the GFW gap-event cross-check — not assumed away.
- **Empty external-label window.** The GFW cross-check is now integrated and exercised, but returned
  no gap, encounter, or loitering events for the recorded LA/LB day. The east-Gulf cohort fixes
  rendezvous/loiter label availability, while gap labels remain incompatible with NOAA's offshore
  receiver coverage.
